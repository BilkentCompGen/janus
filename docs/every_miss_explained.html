Why the repeat merge is the dominant cause

refpos and the graph both apply ref_merge: a run of REPEAT cores is absorbed into its non-repeat flanks, so the bare repeat core never becomes a node. Consequence, measured:

Rebuilding the ruler merge-free proved the split: of the 164, 96 had raw cores that ARE in the graph (my ruler was blind), but 68 had raw cores the graph does not contain — the merge removed them from the caller too.

What this means

  • The pipeline is not losing SVs by accident. Every filter was rebuilt and measured: refine costs 1, --min-mult 3 costs 0 (441M-node rebuild), the strand vote ~1, and the repeat blacklist is net positive. Under 10 records total.
  • The dominant cause is a design choice, not a bug: the (A+X)→(X+B) repeat merge buys a tractable graph and costs 75 records (48% of misses) in satellite/acrocentric regions. That is the trade to argue in the paper — and SVP_SPLIT_REPEATS already exists as the lever to test it.
  • The one real engineering lead is JUNC-FRAGMENTED (14 structural). Novel material at both ends that fails to connect, and it stays broken at mm=1. Novel components have median size 1. Almost all delINVdup.
  • Correction to an earlier claim of mine: --min-mult 3 does NOT cost 0. It costs ~9 junction records — 9 of the 23 JUNC failures recover in the mm=1 rebuild, and the JUNC primitive scores 522 vs 478 at mm=1. My "costs 0" was measured on COPY/LOSS only and was wrong. It is still nearly free for COPY (297→298) and LOSS (353→353), and mm=1 is unusable overall: GAIN fires on 37/37 random loci and clean=0.
  • My metric's remaining fault is 4 records (COPY-INV-FORWARD): small inverted copies whose gain is on the RC side. Narrow and known.
  • Level 4 would attack the 25 CORE-RESOLUTION records — finer cores. It is the only lever for those; refidx_l4.idx and refpos_l4.bin already exist.
