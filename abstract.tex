\begin{abstract}

Somatic structural variants (SVs) drive tumorigenesis across most cancers, yet low variant allele frequencies in subclonal populations often necessitate multi-sample sequencing per patient. Existing somatic SV callers rely on whole-genome read alignments against a single linear reference, incurring high computational costs and reference bias. Here we present \tool, which discovers somatic SVs directly within a personal pangenome, a colored de~Bruijn-like graph of locally consistent parsing (LCP) cores built from matched normal and tumor PacBio HiFi reads. \tool identifies candidate SV sites traversed exclusively by tumor reads without prior alignment, mapping only short read snippets surrounding each site. Across five tumor--normal pairs in the CASTLE panel, \tool led the 18-caller consensus with a mean F1 score of 0.887 (next-best caller: 0.858), widening its lead under low-signal regimes (0.489 versus 0.362 at 5$\times$; 0.683 versus 0.520 at 20\% purity). End to end, \tool analyzed a pair in $\sim$11\,min on 16 threads, which is $\sim$24$\times$ faster than read alignment alone. In a 20-tumor patient simulation, joint graph calling outperformed independent pairwise runs in speed and accuracy, particularly at low depth. By processing multi-sample cohorts jointly and maintaining sensitivity at low coverage and purity, \tool provides an efficient, reference-unbiased framework for cancer genomics.

\tool is available at \url{https://github.com/Muti-Kara/janus}.
\end{abstract}
