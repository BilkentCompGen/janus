For somatic structural variation (SV) in cancer genomes, long-read callers map the tumor and matched normal read sets to a reference carrying neither the donor's germline haplotypes nor the somatic junctions, so SVs are undercalled where the tumor departs from it most. That mapping dominates the runtime and is paid once per pair, yet a case's subclonal architecture, which junctions its tumors share and which belong to one of them, is not a property of any single pair. Here, we present \tool{}, an algorithm that makes the somatic decision before any read is mapped, by Locally Consistent Parsing of the reads themselves. \tool{} counts one normal and $N$ tumor read sets into a single personal pangenome graph of LCP cores, calls a core somatic when its normal count is zero, names the tumors carrying it from the same counts, and reports the result as the donor's own genome carrying each somatic junction as an alternative path. On the four PacBio Revio pairs of the CASTLE panel, \tool{} is comparable to the published calls of Severus, SAVANA and nanomonsv, while mapping 30--100 times less sequence and running 6 times faster end to end than the alignment-first route. That panel's truth set is itself a consensus of eleven published callers, none of them ours; rebuilt with \tool{} as a twelfth member on equal terms, \tool{} scores the highest F1 of the twelve. On a simulated case of one normal and twenty tumors, calling them in one graph is 1.65 times faster than twenty pairwise runs and slightly more accurate.
