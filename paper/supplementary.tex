\section{The truth-set rule}\label{sup:truthrule}

The \texttt{\#\#cmd:} line of two of the four CASTLE truth files records the ensemble rule, the same
in both: a somatic SV enters the truth when at least two of the three platform groups report it and
at least four of the eleven runs do. Every shipped record satisfies both clauses, so neither can be
checked against the records themselves. The records do refute the stricter reading, ``at least two
runs in each of the three groups'', since 176 of HCC1954's 1{,}106 records have support in only two
groups. Re-applying the rule with nothing deleted reproduces 100\% of the shipped records on all four
pairs, which is the consistency check the leave-one-caller-out construction of Table~\ref{tab:loo}
needs, though not a confirmation of the rule itself.

\section{Data and evaluation}\label{sup:data}

The four pairs are the HiFi arm of the CASTLE panel (Cancer Standards Long-read Evaluation),
BioProject PRJNA1086849. Each tumor read set is 185--202 Gbp, about 60--65$\times$ of the human
genome, and the matched normals are of the same order. Their truth sets are the panel's
\texttt{minda\_4\_2} ensembles of eleven caller runs, split into three platform groups: a somatic SV
enters when at least two groups and at least four runs report it (Section~\ref{sup:truthrule}).

That truth is an ensemble of alignment-based callers, and four of the callers we compare \Snape{}
against are members of it. The circularity is large: a \Severus{} vote is present in 93.9--97.3\% of
the records of the four panels once both of its runs are counted, and the share carried by the HiFi
run alone, the one whose calls we score, is 80.8--89.6\%. Every record carries the identifiers of the
runs that support it, so we re-apply the ensemble rule with one caller's votes deleted and score both
sides against the result (Table~\ref{tab:loo}). \Snape{} is a member of none of them.

\section{What the germline check contributes}\label{sup:germ}

Inside a repeat, somatic by exact core sequence is not the same question as somatic: the two samples
may spell one locus with different 217 bp cores, a different number of units in a tandem array or a
different paralogous copy sampled, while the variant itself is shared. On HCC1954 the contigs alone
produce 1,692 calls at a precision of 0.560 and a recall of 0.857; asking the normal at each of their
loci removes 635, of which 632 are false and 3 are true, for a precision of 0.894 at a recall of
0.854. That $+0.334$ is the largest contribution to precision anywhere in the pipeline, against the
$+0.259$ Section~\ref{sup:ablation} measures for the graph itself. Thinning that alignment shows the
check does not saturate, leaving 290 false calls at 3$\times$ depth against 116 at 44$\times$, so the
question is worth asking at full depth, which the selection of Section~\ref{sup:pick} is what makes
affordable.

\section{What the core graph contributes}\label{sup:ablation}

Running the same caller code at the same thresholds over whole-read alignments of HCC1954 instead of
junction contigs, and with the germline check reading the \emph{whole} normal alignment rather than
the selected 1.8--1.9\%, gives 0.635 precision and 0.873 recall against 0.894 and 0.854 for the graph
pipeline: $+0.259$ of precision and $-0.019$ of recall. That arm emitted 1,522 calls against 1,057,
and 556 of them were false against 112.

\textbf{That $+0.259$ is an upper bound}, because the two arms do not match in semantics: the support
floor is the same \emph{number} on both sides and not the same \emph{quantity}. On the graph side
$\theta = 5$ counts distinct contigs, of which a chunk emits at most five, so the floor amounts to
``all five slots filled''; on the whole-read side it counts distinct reads at 60$\times$ depth with no
cap. Part of the gap therefore belongs to the cap rather than to the graph, and two further
asymmetries run the same way, since we give the whole-read arm the whole normal for its germline check
and the two arms differ in the unit \texttt{cluster} merges on. A matched-semantics ablation would
turn this bound into a measurement, and we have not run it.

\section{Recall on orthogonally validated events}\label{sup:pcr}

The 32 PCR-validated records inside HCC1395's truth are the least flattering measurement here:
\Snape{} places a call matching 7 of them. All 32 are calls of a tandem-repeat-specific caller and 26
lie inside a TRF tandem array, which is the population the anchor repeat test of
Section~\ref{sec:call} exists to suppress, because a breakpoint whose flank is a tandem array is where
an aligner puts an indel that is not an event. Relaxing that test from 0.50 to 0.60 recovers nine
insertions on H2009 and takes its false calls from 138 to 345, so on orthogonally validated events
inside tandem repeats this caller recovers roughly a fifth by a deliberate trade. COLO829's 62 events
are worth about $\pm 11$ points at 95\%, so that pair can expose a gross failure and cannot resolve a
small difference.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

We produced every number with \texttt{minimap2} 2.31-r1302, \texttt{samtools} 1.21 and \Minda{}
0.0.2, built with GCC 16.1.1, on one 16-core machine, and we score \Severus{} from the VCFs published
with the panel rather than re-running it, except where stated. The parameter vector used for the
held-out pairs was recorded before either read set was converted, together with the SHA-256 of the
binary (prefix \texttt{f693d4da66a6\allowbreak 508c8b3fe9c64e5958c0}), built from commit
\texttt{aedccc92d82e} plus three uncommitted files whose diff hashes to
\texttt{1dcfb23a9af1\allowbreak af4d1e2b6f570f3c5cad}, on the condition that no result from either
held-out sample would change anything in it. Nothing did.

One property bounds how closely results can be compared: \texttt{junctions} claims its per-chunk
contig budget by an atomic increment across threads, so which reads fill a chunk depends on
scheduling. Every replicate of Section~\ref{sup:repro} starts from a shared \texttt{graph.bin}, so
graph construction itself is untested; the surviving core set is a deterministic function of the input
by construction rather than by measurement, since the merge only unions counts.


\section{Cores, labels and the errors they must survive}\label{sup:cores}

\Snape{} represents a read $r$ by the labels of its cores, so it equates sequence across reads by
label rather than by alignment. Two adjustments make that representation robust to the errors the
data carries. First, cores within a margin $\mu = 10$ bp of either read end are discarded, since a
core cut by a read boundary is not reproducible. Second, the label is computed over the
\emph{homopolymer-compressed} bytes of the core, runs of one repeated base collapsing to a single base
before the parse, with \textsc{MurmurHash64A}~\cite{murmur} as the hash, so that a run-length slip
does not change a locus's identity; core positions are mapped back to raw read coordinates, so
nothing downstream reasons in compressed space. A level-5 core is about 150 parsed bases~\cite{lcpan},
a median of 217 bp of raw read.

That compression is calibrated on the reads rather than assumed. Over 5 Mb of chromosome 1 of
HCC1954's normal, 83.4\% of errors are indels, enriched 2.3$\times$ in homopolymer runs of length
$\geq 3$ against no enrichment for the substitution control, and eighty per cent of them fall inside a
run of length $\geq 2$, where the compression neutralizes them.

\section{Counting the graph}\label{sup:impl}

The parse is not equivariant under reverse complementation, a consequence of the LMAX rule's neighbor
guards, which have no LMIN mirror, so \Snape{} parses each read on both strands and both parses
contribute. Edges are formed for every pair of consecutive retained cores in a read, incremented in
that read's color, and kept when both endpoints survive \eqref{eq:nodes}, giving
$\mathcal{G} = (V, E, (c_N, c_T), (e_N, e_T))$. \Snape{} counts it in one streaming pass over the two
read sets, merging per-worker hash maps into 64 label-sharded global maps and applying
\eqref{eq:nodes} once, at the end, on exact counts.

\Snape{} is implemented in C++17 as a single binary of about 2,300 lines over \Lcptools~\cite{lcpan},
with \texttt{htslib}~\cite{samtools} for alignment input. Read sets are accepted as uncompressed FASTQ
or as a 2-bit packed format the binary reads and writes, and every stage takes either. Each stage
writes one checkpoint, and a run resumes from the last one present.

\section{Measuring an arm's span}\label{sup:bubblerule}

The length of an arm is read off its cores. Consecutive cores of one read overlap, so an arm of $n$
cores with lengths $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_n$ spans $s = \sum_i \ell_i - o\,(n-1)$ bases, where $o$ is the
mean overlap of consecutive cores, a property of the decomposition rather than of the sample: it is
$122.5$ bp on all four CASTLE pairs, on HCC1395 and on COLO829, measured over $1.8$--$2.0\times
10^{9}$ consecutive pairs each, and $122.8$ bp on the simulated reads. \Snape{} measures it on each
run's own reads rather than assuming it.

Order is compared through canonical minimizers on the homopolymer-compressed bases, and the comparison
is ordered rather than set-based, since a junction core is built from the two flanks' material and
shared minimizers alone say nothing. Running the length test in bases rather than in minimizer counts
is what makes $\sigma$ meaningful: a minimizer at these settings is one sample per $16.6$ raw bases
(median over $175{,}753$ bubbles), and the nearest usable threshold in those units would admit
differences of about $200$ bp into the ``local edit'' class. An arm containing a core that no read gave
bytes for has no measurable span, and \Snape{} keeps it rather than judging it.

\section{Which contigs a chunk emits}\label{sup:contigrule}

\Snape{} emits nothing for a window whose achieved anchor is shorter than 300 bp on either side, and
keeps at most five contigs per chunk, named per read and per window. The reduction in sequence to be
aligned tracks the somatic content of each tumor, largest for HCC1937 and smallest for the SV-rich
H2009. The candidate pass that feeds the germline check runs the same calling code at a support floor
of four, one below the reporting floor, so that a call sitting at the boundary still has its normal
reads aligned: a candidate locus too many costs alignment rather than precision.

\section{Reading junctions off the contig alignments}\label{sup:callrule}

Contigs are aligned with \texttt{minimap2 -ax map-hifi -Y -{}-secondary=no}~\cite{minimap2} and sorted
with \texttt{samtools}~\cite{samtools}. \Snape{} reports a junction as a pair of reference sides with
the direction each side leaves the reference in: $d = +1$ when the alignment ends there going right,
$d = -1$ when it starts there going left. That pair is the whole type vocabulary ($(+,-)$ a deletion,
$(-,+)$ a duplication, equal signs an inversion, two different contigs a breakend), so \Snape{} needs
no separate typing step. Junctions come from consecutive alignments of one contig chained in
\emph{query} order, and from CIGAR indels at or above the 50 bp floor.

\Snape{} discards segments below a mapping quality of 10, and alignments whose substitution rate
exceeds 1\%, which with reads at 0.018\% error is not the locus the contig came from; that rate
excludes indel bases, so a contig carrying a real 400 bp deletion does not score as misplaced.

Two \emph{repeat guards} follow, because the aligner emits neither a short inversion nor a
variable-number tandem repeat as a split, walking through both and paying with a dense run of small
indels. \Snape{} separates them by the tandem-repeat content of the window's own bases, the largest
fraction of positions agreeing with the position $p$ before them over $p \in \{1,\dots,6\}$, which is
$\approx 0.25$ for random sequence: a window above 0.40 is not a breakpoint, and a call whose anchor
is above 0.50 is dropped. A short tandem duplication likewise arrives as an insertion, and comparing
the inserted bases with the copy beside them recovers the duplicated interval without a reference.
Section~\ref{sup:splitins} gives the rule that reads an insertion where the pairwise chaining would
read two breakends. A call is positioned at the median of its supporting breakpoints.

\section{The support floor}\label{sup:floor}

The support floor is the pipeline's one free parameter. Sweeping it over the four pairs gives mean F1
0.650, 0.799, 0.808 and 0.792 at floors of three, four, five and six: three is worse than four
everywhere, and four and five split the pairs two apiece but not the margins, four winning HCC1954 by
0.002 and HCC1937 by 0.003 while five wins H2009 by 0.014 and H1437 by 0.028. \Snape{} therefore ships
a floor of five. Section~\ref{sup:supp} gives the per-pair sweep.

\section{Ensemble width and breakpoint tolerance}\label{sup:width}

The panel publishes two looser ensembles for HCC1954, \texttt{minda\_3\_2} and \texttt{minda\_2\_1},
requiring $m$ of the eleven runs and support in $p$ of the three platform groups. Scoring one and the
same pair of call sets against all three moves both callers together: F1 0.870, 0.867 and 0.800 for
\Snape{} against 0.871, 0.861 and 0.798 for \Severus{}, with \Snape{} ahead on the two wider ones.
Re-scoring at \texttt{-{}-tolerance 100} instead of 500 costs both callers absolute F1 (\Snape{} 0.874
to 0.835 on HCC1954, \Severus{} 0.871 to 0.831), leaves the ordering unchanged on every pair and moves
\Snape's margin by less than 0.01, so breakpoint precision is not where the difference between these
callers lies. Widening an ensemble adds hard events and leaves every vote in place, so it cannot
measure the asymmetry that Table~\ref{tab:loo} removes the votes to measure.


\section{Assigning a read to a line}\label{sup:assign}

Every somatic label $x$ carries the support pattern $S(x)$ of \eqref{eq:pattern}, so a read carries a
set of patterns and the lines compatible with it are their intersection. \Snape{} parses the read,
looks up each of its labels that is somatic in the graph, and reports
$\bigcap_{x} S(x)$ over those labels, which collapses to a single line as soon as the read carries one
core private to that line and is the whole set when the read touches no somatic core at all. Three
consequences are worth stating. A read from a region no line has altered is unassignable by
construction, and the fraction of such reads is a property of the somatic burden rather than of the
method. A read spanning a truncal junction narrows the set to the lines below that junction, so
assignment is a partial order rather than a hard label, and reporting the intersection preserves that.
And the lookup costs one parse and $|r|/217$ hash probes per read, with no alignment, so assignment
runs over a pooled read set at parsing speed.

\section{The twenty-line evaluation genome set}\label{sup:phylo}

\textbf{This section describes the evaluation set, which is built; the accuracy of \Snape{} on it is
not reported in this version of the paper.}

The set is one germline genome and a 39-node phylogeny with 20 leaves, all on T2T-CHM13v2.0 (chr1--22
and chrX, gapless, $3{,}054{,}815{,}472$ bp). The germline is a real donor's own variation rather than
a sampled model: HG00733's phased HGSVC3 v1.0 T2T-CHM13 callset, $4{,}778{,}547$ variants
($3{,}761{,}943$ SNV, $993{,}207$ indel, $22{,}589$ SV), written into two haplotype copies of every
chromosome.

Somatic mutations are placed level by level down the tree, so a leaf's variant set is the union of its
root path by construction and the truth is exact. The 20 leaves carry $66{,}070$ mutations in total, a
median of $11{,}330$ each, with SBS and ID channel proportions drawn from COSMIC v3.4 signatures and
six SV classes including reciprocal translocations, inversions and composite events. Two internal
branches carry a subclonal whole-genome duplication, at tree depths 3 and 6, so the leaves fall into
three ploidy families rather than one: ten diploid leaves at $2.10$, six at $2.93$ and four at $3.30$,
copy number being realized as the number of contig copies covering a locus. Reads are simulated per
leaf with Badread's \texttt{pacbio2021} models at $20\times$ of the haploid reference,
$61.1$ Gbp per line.

Three quantities are to be measured on it: per-line junction recall and precision against the written
adjacencies; the Robinson--Foulds distance between the phylogeny implied by the support patterns
\eqref{eq:pattern} and the phylogeny the genomes were built from; and the accuracy of the assignment
of Section~\ref{sup:assign} over the pooled read set, where the generating line of every read is
known.

\section{The two simulations}\label{sup:sim}

Two simulations carry an exact truth. We built tumor genomes from the reference with
insilicoSV~\cite{insilicosv} and simulated 30$\times$ HiFi reads from each and from the unmodified
reference with Badread~\cite{badread}. Because the variants go into the genome before sequencing,
the truth is the set of novel adjacencies the tumor genome contains, and we score at the junction
level: a deletion contributes one junction, an inversion two, and a complex event as many as its
grammar creates.

The first carries 600 structural variants, 118,650 SNVs and 6,580 short indels; at or above the
50 bp floor its truth holds 522 rearrangements (288 deletions, 156 duplications, 78 inversions)
producing 600 novel adjacencies, and 78 insertions, which have no reference interval and which we
match on position and length. \Snape{} recovers 527 of those 600 junctions and 73 of the 78
insertions, and 595 of its 597 calls match a truth junction, a precision of 99.7\%. The second,
which we did not develop against, places 1{,}000 variants of twenty-three grammars, nested and
composite forms such as \texttt{delINVdel}, \texttt{dupINVdup}, \texttt{dDUP\_iDEL} and
\texttt{INV\_rTRA}, producing 1{,}602 novel adjacencies and 130 insertions. At the same defaults, and
from a \texttt{graph.bin} built earlier so that only the stages from \texttt{bubbles} onward are the
frozen binary's, \Snape{} recovers \textbf{1{,}358 of the 1{,}602 junctions (84.8\%) and 123 of the
130 insertions (94.6\%), with 1{,}444 of its 1{,}464 calls matching a truth junction, a precision of
98.6\%}.

Every weak class on the second is a duplication: \texttt{DUP} 64.6\%, \texttt{dDUP} and
\texttt{INV\_dDUP} 59.3\%, \texttt{INV\_DUP} 51.9\%, against 90.4\% for deletions and 95.4\% for
inversions. That reproduces the first simulation's 78.2\% duplication recall on 2.7 times the data,
and the mechanism is the same: the duplicated unit is sequence the genome already carries, so a
tandem copy leaves the fewest cores to work with and a dispersed copy is germline at neither
breakpoint.


\section{The two LCP guarantees}\label{sup:defs}

\begin{definition}[Partition consistency]
Given an input string $S$ and two occurrences $X_i$, $X_j$ of the same substring, $X_i$ and $X_j$
are partitioned in the same way, with the possible exception of their left and right margins.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Labeling consistency]
Cores that consist of the same characters are assigned the same label.
\end{definition}


\section{The somatic-decision proposition}\label{sup:prop}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:somatic}
Let a tumor haplotype join two loci whose sequences the normal also carries, with at least the LCP
window of flanking sequence on each side of the join, and let both alleles be sequenced to at least
$\theta$ reads. Then \emph{(i)} the cores strictly inside the two flanks are the cores the normal
produces there, and \emph{(ii)} a core spanning the join receives a label that no normal read
produces, unless the same adjacency, or a sequence with the same compressed bases, occurs elsewhere
in the normal.
\end{proposition}

Part (i) is partition consistency applied to the flanks, whose content the join leaves unchanged;
part (ii) is labeling consistency read in the contrapositive, since a label is a function of the
core's bases and a core over bases the normal never sequences cannot carry a label the normal
produced. The exception in (ii) occurs in practice: in a tandem array or a segmental duplication the two samples
may spell one locus with different cores while carrying the same allele, which is why \Snape{} puts the
surviving candidates to the normal reads once more in reference space
(Section~\ref{sec:call}).


\section{The split-insertion rule}\label{sup:splitins}

Most short insertions are another copy of sequence the genome already has, and the aligner is free to
place that copy at its source locus rather than write it as a CIGAR insertion, so the contig arrives
as three segments whose outer two are adjacent in the reference. \Snape{} emits the stranded query
sequence as one insertion instead of the two junctions chaining would produce. Taking its length as
every query base between the two anchors also covers a middle segment the mapping-quality gate has
already discarded. The rule fires on 128 of H2009's 537,144 aligned segments and is worth three
insertions there at no cost in false calls.

\section{Where the anchor requirement binds}\label{sup:anchor}

Halving the anchor a read must achieve, from 300 bp to 150 (Section~\ref{sec:contigs}), rejects
46,393 fewer read windows on H2009 and returns 1,448 more populated chunks for them, 0.8\% more, of
which 775 reach the five-contig cap. The shape of the distribution does not move: 58.5\% of populated
chunks sit at the cap both before and after, and the bins below it absorb the rest. Scored, the
change moves five of the 925 truth events into reach and one out, which a paired exact test does not
separate from noise ($p = 0.22$). It is not in the shipped defaults, because we froze the parameter
vector before running the held-out pairs and a CASTLE result is not allowed to move it afterwards.
The exchange rate is the useful quantity: thirty-two rejected windows have to be recovered to gain
one populated chunk. The per-bin totals cannot separate a promotion of a chunk that already had a
contig from an arrival, so some of the 1,448 may be promotions.

\section{Tumor-only adjacencies between germline cores}\label{sup:adjacency}

An adjacency can in principle be tumor-only while both of its endpoint cores are germline, in which
case the junction leaves no somatic core and the chunk definition is blind to it. Such adjacencies
are present in the graph as edges with a tumor count and no normal count, and 282,455 of them exist
in HCC1954's graph. Over 1,462 truth loci on the four panels, an adjacency of that kind is present at
0.7--3.0\% of loci and is no more common at the events \Snape{} misses than at the events it finds
(Fisher exact $p = 0.7$ for insertions, $0.79$ for deletions), and not one of the 141 loci at which
no somatic core forms carries one. Those loci are instead large deletions, median 3.7 kb, in the size
range where \Snape{} has no recall deficit at all.

\section{A depth-based bubble rule, and why it is not shipped}\label{sup:depth}

Never dropping a bubble whose somatic arm the tumor traversed at least $T$ times recovers all 61 of
the H2009 truth insertions that have a somatic core, for 558{,}714 surviving cores at its best
setting, while the length threshold of \eqref{eq:bubble} recovers the same 61 for 547{,}180. At the
operating point \eqref{eq:bubble} ships at, 145k surviving cores, the depth rule costs $3.9\times$ as
many cores for the same recovery.
